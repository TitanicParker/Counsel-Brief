# Clinical Facts Clarifying the Management Frame

**Purpose:** This document does not answer or rebut the independent reader. It uses the clinical record to make explicit several facts that are easy to miss when the allegation and discharge-artefact analysis are read without the complete longitudinal file.

The point is not to strengthen the allegation by assertion. It is to show what the treating neurologist's own records say about the origin, continuity and later defence of the management approach.

---

## 1. The July 2017 discharge account is not merely one historical source among many

The long July 2017 discharge account is the treating neurologist's own extended synthesis of the admission.

It does several things in the same document:

- records the young-onset Parkinsonian syndrome;
- records the dystonic-looking gait;
- records the structural foot pathology;
- records the chronic painful/burning foot phenomena as possibly separate or possibly neuropathic and leaves their relationship **"Not clear"**;
- records senior neurological involvement in future dopaminergic treatment;
- records a deliberate intention to avoid levodopa/dopamine agonists for as long as possible;
- sets out the staged treatment plan;
- directs the feet to Podiatry;
- and later incorporates the preceding account into the formal recommendations.

The relevant issue is therefore not whether two unrelated facts happened to coexist somewhere in the chart.

They coexist inside the neurologist's own synthesis of **what the admission meant and how the patient would be managed next**.

The document says:

> **"We were likely try and avoid these for as long as possible."**

about levodopa/dopamine agonists.

In the same account it says that the leg/foot symptoms, including corns, calluses and burning:

> **"may have been completely separate or perhaps a secondary peripheral neuropathy"**

and concludes:

> **"Not clear."**

The document then requests outpatient Podiatry.

That does not prove that levodopa deferral caused the foot classification, or vice versa. It does establish that both propositions were operative inside the same forward management account.

---

## 2. The clinician later treated the 2017 material as the foundation explaining his management

This is especially important.

In his 25 March 2025 response to the formal complaint, Dr Monaghan did not distance himself from the 2017 account or treat it as an obsolete historical document.

He enclosed his working clinical file and relied on the earlier letters as the explanation of what had been done.

He wrote that:

> **"I feel my own clinical reasoning will be evident to the reviewer"**

and later said that the purpose of his long letters was:

> **"clear documentation in terms of records for knowing how decision were made and when and by who"**

He then stated:

> **"I hope that the information contained within (In this account and in all of the letters from 2017) will be able to deliver the answers that Mr Moloney requests."**

The 2025 complaint response therefore matters for more than the merits of the complaint.

It identifies the older letters — including the July 2017 material — as the treating neurologist's own documentary explanation of the management history.

The 2017 account is not being retrospectively selected by the patient as an artificial "foundation". The treating neurologist himself returned to it in 2025 as the documentary foundation from which his decisions were to be understood.

---

## 3. In 2025 the neurologist expressly re-adopted the original levodopa strategy

The 2025 response quotes the 2017 passage again:

> **"Liam has a good understanding of why we wished to delay the commencement of levodopa or dopamine agonist type treatments at least for now."**

It continues:

> **"It is possible that he will do very well with levodopa and in a sustainable way whenever it is started. Indeed in time to come - we may regret that we did not start this earlier."**

For the 2025 complaint response, Dr Monaghan added bolding and underlining to that historical passage.

He then summarised the longitudinal strategy in his own present-tense retrospective words:

> **"In essence we have known that Mr Moloney has Parkinsons; we have known it would be Sinemet or other dopamine agonist responsive; we have tried to spare commencing those for some time..."**

This is an unusually clear statement of continuity.

The 2017 restraint was not merely an isolated decision that disappeared from the later record. In 2025 the treating neurologist himself described the later management as the continuation of that same strategy.

---

## 4. The 2025 response also joins levodopa restraint to the management of the foot pain

The most important clarification is that the 2025 response does not discuss levodopa restraint in one compartment and the foot pain in another completely unrelated compartment.

It explains them together.

Dr Monaghan wrote that:

> **"we have had a clear understanding for quite some time that Sinemet does help his pain."**

He explained his reluctance to increase Sinemet rapidly by reference to dyskinesia and other long-term risks in a younger patient.

He then said that pregabalin/Lyrica had been introduced for the neuropathic pain:

> **"(of whatever cause) in part to try to spare further Sinemet increase"**

and acknowledged that the Lyrica treatment had:

> **"incomplete effect at maximum dose"**

before describing the later Sinemet CR escalation and the resulting:

> **"profound relief currently."**

This is direct evidence that the two parts of the alleged management frame were not merely adjacent historical facts.

By 2025 the treating neurologist himself described a management logic in which:

- Sinemet was known to help the pain;
- Sinemet escalation was deliberately restrained because of long-term dopaminergic risks;
- pregabalin was used for the pain partly to avoid further Sinemet increase;
- and the pain itself remained capable of being attributed to a separate underlying cause.

Whether that management was clinically correct is an expert question.

But the existence of the integrated management logic is documentary fact.

---

## 5. The pain response was accepted; what remained unchanged was what the response was allowed to mean

The 2025 complaint response is particularly clear on this point.

Dr Monaghan did not deny that Parkinson medication improved the feet.

He wrote:

> **"He knows very well that once the Sinemet hits his system that the feet feel better from a pain perspective."**

He said:

> **"we have completely accepted the beneficial effect"**

of Sinemet on the experience of pain.

But he maintained that this did not identify the source of that pain. His model was that Parkinsonism could magnify or diminish pain:

> **"of whatever cause."**

He used the analogy of old ankle or leg fractures: Sinemet might make them feel better without causing the original injury.

That is the frame in its mature form.

The local fact is accepted:

> **Sinemet changes the feet and the pain.**

The global causal question remains open:

> **the pain may still arise from another underlying cause.**

This is why the question of "local update without global update" is not created by the patient's later language. It is visible in the treating neurologist's own explanation of the treatment response.

---

## 6. The 2025 "if" is not an isolated phrase

The words:

> **"If there is a degree of peripheral neuropathy here..."**

should not be used alone as proof that July 2017 remained unchanged for eight years.

The intervening record has to establish continuity.

But the phrase is stronger when read inside the 25 March complaint response because Dr Monaghan himself uses it as evidence that he had not been completely anchored to alcohol-related neuropathy.

He says, in effect, that the conditional wording demonstrates openness to other causes.

That is important because it establishes what the proposition was in 2025:

**peripheral neuropathy remained possible, conditional and available as an underlying explanation.**

It had not become an established diagnosis.

At the same time, Sinemet benefit was fully accepted.

The question for the longitudinal record is therefore not whether the 2025 word "if" magically proves continuity. It is whether the pathway from July 2017 to March 2025 ever contains a global reclassification that replaces the original unresolved painful-foot question.

---

## 7. April 2018 is important because the frame received new information and continued

The October 2017 procyclidine withdrawal first generated strong contemporaneous evidence that neurological treatment changed the feet, gait, tension and function together.

By April 2018 that was no longer merely patient-origin evidence.

Neurology itself recorded that, after stopping procyclidine, the **first thing** to return rapidly was the outside-left-foot phenomenon, while the patient also became more "shifty" and "unrelaxed".

The same April 2018 review said that he could be:

> **"very much better"**

with levodopa or another dopamine agonist, while maintaining the previously stated intention not to use them for quite some time.

This does not prove that a levodopa trial was mandatory.

It does establish that the original plan encountered new information in precisely the domain that had been left unresolved.

The evidential question is therefore simple:

> **What did that information do to the July "Not clear" relationship?**

If the record contains a global reconsideration, it should be identified.

If it contains only recognition of the local treatment effect while the underlying painful-foot classification remains separate or conditional, that is the factual basis for the allegation.

---

## 8. The complaint response substantially clarifies "ownership" of the frame

A reader who sees only the allegation and the discharge-artefact document may reasonably wonder whether the present analysis has retrospectively coupled unrelated parts of the record.

The 25 March 2025 response materially reduces that uncertainty.

The treating neurologist himself:

- points the reviewer back to his historical letters;
- says those letters document how and why decisions were made;
- quotes and highlights the 2017 levodopa-deferral rationale;
- summarises the later course as deliberate sparing of Sinemet;
- acknowledges long-standing knowledge that Sinemet helps the pain;
- explains Lyrica partly as a means of sparing additional Sinemet;
- acknowledges incomplete Lyrica effect;
- acknowledges profound Sinemet CR relief;
- and nevertheless retains a model in which the pain can arise from another, still conditional, peripheral cause.

This does not establish negligence.

It does clarify the object being investigated.

The management frame is not merely something the present allegation has projected backwards onto the record.

**Its core logic is described and defended by the treating neurologist himself in 2025 by reference to the documentary architecture he created from 2017 onward.**

---

## 9. What the complete record now has to establish

The remaining task is factual and longitudinal.

The record should be used to establish, without forcing every foot symptom into one category:

1. which July 2017 foot phenomena were structural;
2. which were dynamic motor/postural phenomena;
3. which painful phenomena later changed with neurological treatment or OFF state;
4. when clinicians recognised those changes;
5. how those observations were classified at each stage;
6. whether peripheral neuropathy was ever established;
7. every occasion on which the overarching relationship between chronic painful feet and the movement disorder was expressly reconsidered;
8. and whether any such reconsideration replaced the July 2017 **"Not clear"** relationship with a materially different global model.

That is the proof layer.

The question is no longer whether the allegation can be made more emphatic.

It is whether the complete clinical record demonstrates the continuity, modification or abandonment of the management frame the neurologist himself described.
