# DISCHARGE ARTEFACT — FINDINGS RELEVANT TO THE ALLEGATION

**Status:** Working evidential companion to `06_THE_ALLEGATION.md`.

**Purpose:** This document is not a second allegation and is not a substitute for the complete sentence-by-sentence audit in `07_FOUNDATIONAL_DISCHARGE_LETTER_IMPEACHMENT.md`. It extracts only the features of the July 2017 discharge artefact that materially bear on the current management-frame allegation.

The central point is simple:

> **The completed discharge document was not a contemporaneous snapshot of the moment of physical discharge. It was a delayed, patient-facing synthesis that consolidated the clinicians' account of the admission into a forward management plan after the patient says he had already challenged that plan.**

That fact changes how the document can safely be used as evidence of what was settled, accepted, understood or uncontested at the bedside.

---

## 1. What the document says it is

The long neurological letter expressly describes its own function.

It says that part of its purpose is for supply to the patient and that it is intended:

> **“to consolidate a lot of discussions which we have had.”**

It also says it is intended to help the patient:

> **“with debriefing”**

and:

> **“to set out for him a clear plan for the gradual titration onto different treatment modalities.”**

Those statements matter because they make the artefact more than a passive clinical note. The author presents it as a retrospective consolidation of what the admission meant and as the document through which the patient and GP are to understand what happens next.

The Plan/Recommendations section later states simply:

> **“As per above.”**

The preceding narrative is therefore not merely descriptive background. It is incorporated wholesale into the formal plan.

For the present allegation, this is the first important documentary mechanism:

> **narrative becomes management.**

If a material disagreement or alternative course is absent from the narrative being incorporated, it is also absent from the formal plan as transmitted forward.

---

## 2. The management frame contained two distinct but durable components

The document records a genuine young-onset Parkinsonian syndrome and a deliberate treatment policy. It states that the “greatest of care” had been advised with senior neurological colleagues concerning the later use of dopamine agonists or levodopa and says:

> **“We were likely try and avoid these for as long as possible.”**

The document nevertheless accepts that these treatments would probably be required eventually. In the later patient-facing discussion it says that the patient might do very well and sustainably with levodopa and adds:

> **“we may regret that we did not start this earlier.”**

The policy was therefore not based on levodopa being irrelevant. It was an intentional policy of delay, adopted despite an acknowledged possibility of substantial benefit and possible later regret.

At the same time, the feet were placed into a separate unresolved category. The examination itself records:

> **“The gait is strikingly abnormal.”**

and:

> **“footdrop appearance somewhat suggestive of a dystonic posture - NOT FOOT DROP.”**

It also records pes planus and hammertoes. Yet the later synthesis says that the leg/foot symptoms, including corns, calluses and burning:

> **“may have been completely separate or perhaps a secondary peripheral neuropathy”**

and ends the causal question:

> **“Not clear.”**

Podiatry is then the practical destination for the feet.

The allegation therefore reads the completed plan as containing two operative commitments:

> **Parkinsonism would be managed neurologically under a policy of prolonged dopaminergic restraint; the chronic painful feet would remain causally unresolved and practically separate while their relationship to the neurological disorder stayed “Not clear.”**

---

## 3. “Markedly divorced” is a phenomenological distinction, not a causal concession

The discharge synthesis says:

> **“he has in his own mind - markedly divorced the feelings of tension in the legs ... from the ‘corns and callouses’ issues and pains in the feet.”**

This sentence is important and should be retained, not avoided.

It records something the patient himself had learned from his own history: the internal feeling of bodily/leg tension and the pain associated with the feet did not feel like the same experience. The account connects that distinction with the fact that reduced time weight-bearing after leaving bar work had reduced foot pain while the internal tension persisted.

But the sentence does not establish three further propositions:

- that the two experiences had been proved to have unrelated causes;
- that neurological state could not be contributing to foot posture, loading, clawing or pain around genuine structural pathology;
- or that the patient accepted Podiatry as a sufficient answer to the feet at discharge.

The patient’s Protest is directly relevant because his account is that, after all of this discussion, he specifically objected that the feet remained unresolved.

Accordingly, **“markedly divorced” cannot safely be converted from a description of how two symptom experiences felt into proof that the patient accepted the management partition later embodied in the final letter.**

---

## 4. The document itself portrays the plan as increasingly firm

Several passages describe the admission as having produced a settled forward architecture.

The author says that the extended admission:

> **“has all suited us and [the patient] very well”**

and that its longer duration:

> **“has helped us have a firmer footing going forward for having a plan of how things will be introduced.”**

The patient is repeatedly described as highly intelligent, exceptionally insightful, capable of retaining information and questioning appropriately. Later the document says:

> **“[Patient] has a good understanding of why we wished to delay the commencement of levodopa or dopamine agonist type treatments at least for now.”**

There is no necessary contradiction between understanding the clinicians’ rationale and disagreeing with part of the resulting plan. Indeed, the document’s repeated description of the patient as someone who questions “very appropriately with exceptional insight” is entirely compatible with the Protest as described by him.

These passages therefore establish strong evidence of **the author’s retrospective representation of the admission as having produced a coherent and understood plan**.

They are much weaker evidence that the patient had accepted every causal and management consequence of that plan, particularly the disposition of the feet.

---

## 5. The Protest changes the meaning of the final synthesis

On the patient’s direct account, before physical discharge he objected that the feet remained unresolved and asked for the supervising neurologist. His NCT / bald-tyres analogy did not deny the newly identified Parkinsonian syndrome. It challenged the adequacy of leaving the original disabling foot problem unresolved while proceeding to discharge.

That event created a fork:

> **continue with the management frame already formed; or return the unresolved feet and the patient’s objection to senior neurological review before closure.**

The completed discharge artefact contains the pathway that continued. It does not contain the fork.

That omission is especially significant because of the document’s own claimed functions: consolidation, debriefing, explanation and plan-setting.

The material effect alleged is therefore not merely:

> “a conversation is missing from the notes.”

It is:

> **the final synthesis preserves the plan that was followed without preserving the fact that the patient says the sufficiency of that plan had been challenged before closure.**

This is the sense in which the omission can be said to **harden** the management frame. The word describes documentary effect, not motive.

---

## 6. The discharge artefact is temporally layered

The document cannot safely be treated as though its complete narrative existed in final form at the moment the patient physically left hospital on 11 July 2017.

Its own text proves that it remained open afterwards.

It contains:

> **“ADDENDUM at Transcription finalisation 2017-07-13th_21:41hrs”**

and then the author writes:

> **“I do apologise for the delay in getting this out”**

before incorporating the DaT scan performed on 13 July and adding interpretation of that result.

The surviving artefact carries later final consultant sign-off on 17 July 2017.

The consequence is methodological, not accusatory:

> **the final document is a temporally layered retrospective synthesis, not a unitary contemporaneous bedside record.**

Therefore statements in the completed artefact about what the patient understood, how well the admission suited him, how firm the plan had become, or what constituted the final recommendations cannot simply authenticate themselves as facts already settled at physical discharge.

Their evidential value depends on provenance: when the proposition was formed, what source underlay it, whether it predates or postdates the Protest, and whether contrary material was available before finalisation.

Anything generated or selected after the Protest but before completion of the final artefact is therefore legitimately open to that provenance inquiry. This does not make the material false. It means **the finished document cannot be used as its own proof that the admission had been uncontested before it was finished.**

---

## 7. The artefact was open to later information but not to the alleged fork

The temporal layering matters for another reason.

The document was demonstrably capable of absorbing information arriving after physical discharge. The 13 July DaT scan was added and interpreted. The author expressly anticipated later investigative results and used the developing document to consolidate them.

Against that background, the absence of the Protest cannot be explained merely by saying that the final letter was a fixed contemporaneous note written before the event.

It was not.

The safe evidential proposition is narrower:

> **the Protest, on the patient’s evidence, was known before the final artefact was completed; the artefact remained capable of amendment and later supplementation; yet the completed patient-facing consolidation contains the chosen management pathway and not the patient’s challenge to it.**

Why that occurred is a separate question. The documentary consequence is visible without deciding motive.

---

## 8. Selective integration is visible inside the document itself

The discharge material contains several pieces of the later integrated phenotype, but they are not joined in the synthesis.

The examination contains the dystonic-looking lower-limb/gait finding:

> **“somewhat suggestive of a dystonic posture - NOT FOOT DROP.”**

The genetic discussion then specifically considers PRKN/PARK2 because of very young onset, akinetic rigidity, absence of a dominant family history, marked symmetry and preserved cognition.

The dystonic lower-limb finding is not included in that patient-specific PRKN feature list. By contrast, the same section expressly links PARK9 to:

> **“the neuropathy question.”**

The importance of this is not that the document lacked either dystonia or PRKN. It had both.

The importance is structural:

> **dystonia remains attached to the gait; PRKN remains attached to the Parkinsonian/genetic analysis; the chronic painful feet remain attached to separate/neuropathy/“Not clear”.**

The document therefore contains the components of a more integrated question while preserving them in different explanatory compartments.

This is consistent with the management-frame allegation and with the later finding of **local update without global update**. It does not by itself establish negligence or motive.

---

## 9. What can properly be alleged from this document

Read with the patient’s Protest, the discharge artefact supports a coherent allegation about **management of uncertainty and documentary hardening**:

The admission produced a deliberate treatment strategy and an unresolved foot partition. The patient says he challenged the sufficiency of that partition before discharge. The final document was completed afterwards, expressly for consolidation, debriefing and plan-setting. It incorporates the narrative into the formal recommendations through “As per above”, records the patient as understanding the treatment rationale, and presents the admission as having created a firmer footing for the future plan. It does not record the Protest or the alternative path it created.

The completed artefact therefore cannot simply be treated as neutral contemporaneous proof that the plan it records was the only or uncontested conclusion of the admission.

Its proper evidential role is more exact:

> **It is strong evidence of the management frame the neurologist ultimately chose to record, explain and transmit. It is not, by itself, complete proof of the contested bedside history from which that frame emerged.**

That distinction is central to the current allegation.

---

## 10. What this finding does not establish

This document does not establish why the Protest was omitted. It does not establish dishonest intent. It does not establish that every foot symptom was caused by dystonia or PRKN. It does not establish that Podiatry was inappropriate. It does not establish that a levodopa trial was medically mandatory in July 2017.

Those questions require other evidence and, for clinical standard-of-care questions, independent expert opinion.

The narrower finding is enough:

> **A management frame containing deliberate levodopa deferral and unresolved separation of the chronic painful feet was turned into a durable, patient-facing plan by a discharge artefact that was completed after the patient says he had challenged that frame. The artefact omitted the challenge and therefore omitted the alternative path from the history it transmitted.**

The later procyclidine, OFF-state and levodopa evidence belongs in `06_THE_ALLEGATION.md`, where it tests what happened to that frame after 2017.
